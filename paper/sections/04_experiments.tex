\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Models.}
We evaluate TDU on three autoregressive language models of varying scale:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GPT-2} (124M parameters, 12 layers)~\citep{radford2019language}
    \item \textbf{Pythia-410M} (410M parameters, 24 layers)~\citep{biderman2023pythia}
    \item \textbf{Pythia-1B} (1B parameters, 16 layers)~\citep{biderman2023pythia}
\end{itemize}
All models are accessed via the TransformerLens library~\citep{nanda2023transformerlens}, which provides standardized hook interfaces for activation interventions.

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use the CounterFact benchmark~\citep{meng2022locating}, which contains approximately 20,000 factual associations of the form (subject, relation, object). We randomly sample 50 facts for our experiments, ensuring diversity across relation types.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We measure layer-wise attribution via gradient norms and validate causal importance via ablation studies. For each fact and layer, we compute:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Gradient Norm:} $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}} \mathcal{L}\|_2$ measuring sensitivity of the loss to activations at layer $\ell$.
    \item \textbf{Ablation Delta:} Change in loss when zeroing the layer's contribution, measuring causal importance.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:layer_attr} shows the mean gradient attribution scores across 50 facts for each model.

\paragraph{GPT-2 (12 layers).}
Layer 2 shows the highest attribution with mean gradient norm 62.67 ($\pm$ 13.76), followed by layer 10 at 60.21 ($\pm$ 16.25). The ablation delta at layer 2 is 11.71, confirming strong causal influence on factual recall.

\paragraph{Pythia-410M (24 layers).}
Attribution is distributed across the deeper architecture, with layer 23 showing the highest score (14.33 $\pm$ 3.82). Later layers (21, 22, 23) contribute most significantly, with mean ablation delta of 8.12 at layer 23.

\paragraph{Pythia-1B (16 layers).}
Layer 3 shows the highest attribution (39.24 $\pm$ 9.15), with layers 0 and 14-15 also contributing significantly. The ablation delta of 10.15 at layer 3 confirms causal responsibility for factual recall.

Table~\ref{tab:layer_stats} and Table~\ref{tab:top_layers} summarize the layer attribution results. Key findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Factual encoding is sparse:} 2-3 layers account for the majority of attribution, enabling efficient targeted editing.
    
    \item \textbf{Layer patterns vary by architecture:} GPT-2 shows bimodal distribution (layers 2 and 10), while Pythia models concentrate attribution differently.
    
    \item \textbf{Ablation validates attribution:} High gradient norms correlate with large ablation deltas, confirming causal responsibility.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion}

These layer attribution results provide the foundation for TDU's targeted intervention strategy. By identifying which layers encode specific facts, we can apply surgical rank-one projections to remove knowledge while minimizing collateral damage to unrelated capabilities.

Future work will evaluate TDU against baseline unlearning methods (ROME, MEMIT, gradient ascent) on standard metrics including efficacy, paraphrase generalization, and specificity scores.
